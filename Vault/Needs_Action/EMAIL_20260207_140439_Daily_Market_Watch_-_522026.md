Australia Balance of Trade

In November 2025, Australia’s trade surplus, the profit made from selling more goods to other countries than it buys from them, fell to AUD 2.94 billion. This was much lower than the AUD 4.9 billion that experts had predicted. The main reason for this decline was a drop in exports, particularly in minerals and metals. Sales to the United States fell sharply by 10.5% due to new trade taxes (tariffs), while shipments to other major partners like India and South Korea also decreased. Meanwhile, Australians continued to buy many goods from abroad, reaching a record high in imports as people prepared for the holiday season.

This data is a "Bearish" signal for the Australian Dollar (AUD). When a country sells fewer goods globally, there is less demand for its currency, which can cause its value to drop against other currencies like the USD or EUR. Additionally, because the result was significantly lower than what the market expected, we may see increased price swings (volatility) in the markets. Moving forward, traders should watch if these new tariffs continue to hurt Australian sales, as a shrinking trade surplus often puts downward pressure on both the currency and the stocks of large mining companies.

BoE Interest Rate Decision

The Bank of England is expected to keep interest rates steady at 3.75% this week. Although the Bank wants to lower rates in the future to help the economy grow, it is currently moving very slowly because inflation in the UK is at 3.4%, which is higher than in other major countries. Policymakers are particularly worried about high salaries (wage growth); if people earn more money and spend it, prices may stay high, making it difficult to reach their 2% inflation target. Therefore, the Bank is choosing to "wait and see" for more data before making it cheaper to borrow.

This means the British Pound (GBP) will likely stay strong in the short term because higher interest rates make a currency more attractive to investors. However, you should watch for the Bank’s "forward guidance"—the hints they give about future moves. If the Bank suggests that interest rate cuts are coming sooner than expected, the Pound could lose value quickly. On the other hand, if they emphasize that rates must stay high to fight inflation, it could support the currency but put pressure on the UK stock market, as high rates make it more expensive for companies to operate.

ECB Interest Rate Decision

The European Central Bank (ECB) is expected to keep interest rates at their current level this Thursday. While interest rates usually stay the same to keep the economy stable, inflation in Europe has recently dropped to 1.7%, which is below the Bank’s official 2% target. This low inflation is partly caused by cheaper goods arriving from China and a very strong Euro. When a currency like the Euro becomes too strong, it makes European products more expensive for the rest of the world to buy, which can hurt the economy. Because of this, the Bank is carefully watching to see if it needs to lower rates later this year to keep Europe competitive.

The main focus will be on the Bank’s "tone" during their announcement. If the ECB signals that the Euro is too strong and that interest rate cuts are coming soon, the value of the Euro (EUR) will likely fall. This is known as a "Dovish" signal. However, if the Bank stays quiet about future cuts and focuses on the economy’s strength, the Euro could continue to rise. For the stock market, any hint of lower interest rates in the future is usually "Bullish" (positive), as cheaper borrowing costs help companies grow. As a new trader, you should watch for price swings in the Euro immediately following the Bank's press conference.
